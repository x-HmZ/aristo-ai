# Stay on fal, upgrade Tripo, test one image swap

**Stay on fal.ai. No provider switch, direct API or self-host is supported by evidence as both cheaper and no worse in quality.** Going direct to Google saves about 11% on Nano Banana Pro ($0.134 vs $0.15) and BFL direct matches fal's Kontext price, so the savings are cents per concept. Concepts are generated once and cached in Supabase, so total spend is small anyway ([Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), [BFL pricing](https://bfl.ai/pricing)). Two changes are worth making. First, move image-to-3D from Tripo v2.5 to Tripo H3.1: it costs the same $0.30 standard-texture price on fal and tops the only public blind-vote boards found ([fal Tripo H3.1](https://fal.ai/models/tripo3d/h3.1/image-to-3d/llms.txt), [Top3D.AI](https://www.top3d.ai/leaderboard)). This also removes the retirement risk, since Scenario says v2.5 stops working on its platform on 1 Dec 2026. Second, run a small A/B on the labelled teaching image. Several cheaper models tie Nano Banana Pro at exact spelling, but nobody has measured diagram layout or "no extra text" behaviour, so no swap should happen on benchmark evidence alone. The age-terms issue is a compliance question that applies to every provider and needs a written answer from fal. Estimated A/B cost is about $6 to $8, needing the owner's go-ahead.

**Step 1: do not swap on benchmark evidence.** Eleven models, including Nano Banana Pro, Nano Banana 2.1, GPT Image 2.5 Flare, Recraft V4.1, Qwen Image 3.0 and Grok Imagine 2.0, render every required string exactly (5.00/5) on the img.ly benchmark ([img.ly](https://img.ly/ai-benchmarks/findings/text-reliability/)). This benchmark is vendor-run, and its prompts include product labels and a labelled bar chart, not teaching diagrams. Nano Banana Pro also ranks only 18th on Artificial Analysis general preference, at the highest price tier ([AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). Spelling no longer separates the field, so the live differentiators are label placement, leader-line correctness, and not adding unrequested titles or paragraphs, and none of these has a public measurement. Because the owner will not accept quality loss, the burden of proof sits with the challenger: NB 2.1 is the first candidate (same family, about $0.04), with GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Recraft V4.1 as second candidates. Seedream 5.x scores well in vendor tables but has anecdotal failures on complex infographics, so it is low priority. Direct-to-Google batch pricing (50% off) is irrelevant because lessons are interactive ([Batch API](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/batch-api)).

**Step 2: nothing to do.** FLUX 3 Image Klein ($0.009) and Z-Image Turbo ($0.005 per MP) are cheap but cost more than Schnell, and neither is tested for this task ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein), [fal Z-Image](https://fal.ai/docs/model-api-reference/image-generation-api/z-image-turbo)). A screening sheet costs under $0.50 and fits the existing practice, but the expected gain is zero cost savings, so it is optional.

## Tripo v2.5 may be retired: move to H3.1 now

Scenario's October 2026 guide says Tripo 2.5 "stops working on December 1, 2026" on its platform and recommends 3.1 ([Scenario](https://help.scenario.com/articles/1263568892-comparing-generative-3d-models)). That is Scenario's catalogue, not Tripo's or fal's announcement, so whether fal's `tripo3d/tripo/v2.5` endpoint dies on the same date is unverified. Tripo's own docs say v2.5 and v3.0 remain available alongside v3.1 ([Tripo docs](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/models/v3-1)). The prudent reading is that v2.5 is a generation behind and the retirement risk is real. Since H3.1 has no price penalty on fal ($0.20 untextured, $0.30 standard texture, $0.40 detailed texture, +$0.05 quad), the safe action is to migrate to `tripo3d/h3.1/image-to-3d` and keep v2.5 only as a fallback until it is confirmed live.

Quality evidence favours the move but is thin. Tripo v3.1 leads Top3D.AI's textured board (1094 Elo vs Rodin Gen 2.5 at 1066 and Meshy 7.1 at 1057) and geometry board (1146, 84.7% win rate), but the gaps to rivals are 30 to 40 Elo points on a small aggregator with undisclosed affiliation ([Top3D.AI](https://www.top3d.ai/leaderboard/seasons/sep-26)). A vendor paper for a competitor found Tripo 3.1 misshaped lettering and hallucinated inscriptions at 32%, which matters if source images contain markings ([arXiv 2610.11685](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11685v1)); the clean unlabelled source rule already avoids this. No evidence exists on cells, organs, thin parts or transparent objects for any provider, so a 4-object test on the real concepts is the only valid check. Parameters differ in H3.1 (`face_limit`, `texture_alignment`, `pbr` default on), so budget a small code change and one regression pass over cached assets. The $0.40 HD multiview price on v2.5 has no confirmed H3.1 equivalent; H3.1 accepts multiview input natively at the same listed credit tiers ([Tripo docs](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/models/v3-1)).

## Direct and self-host options save cents and add work

Google direct is $0.134 for NB Pro (about $0.016 saved per image) and $0.067 for NB2 at 1K (about $0.013 saved), with paid-tier data not used for product improvement ([Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Against that sit a new SDK, base64 outputs, no documented EU region on the Gemini API, and an under-18 clause that is more direct than fal's. Vertex lists Gemini 3 Pro Image in europe-west4, but forum reports describe preview models served only from the global endpoint, and Vertex per-image pricing could not be read ([Vertex locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/locations)). BFL direct offers Kontext Pro at the same $0.04 and an EU-only endpoint (`api.eu.bfl.ai`) whose generated URLs expire in 10 minutes ([BFL integration guide](https://docs.bfl.ai/api_integration/integration_guidelines)); useful only if EU routing becomes a requirement. Tripo direct shows no evidence of being cheaper: credits are $0.01 each and H3.1 standard texture appears to be 45 credits, or $0.45 ([Tripo developers](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/), [PoYo reseller](https://poyo.ai/models/tripo-h31-3d), unverified).

Self-hosting is not worth it. The one clean open model, TRELLIS.2 (MIT), needs a 24 GB+ GPU on Linux with a CUDA-heavy build ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Arithmetic in this report puts Modal cost at roughly $0.06 to $0.15 per model including cold starts, against $0.30, so saving tens of dollars a month for a solo developer's engineering time. The official export path depends on NVIDIA libraries restricted to non-commercial research, and the ComfyUI write-up says closed generators still beat TRELLIS.2 ([ComfyUI blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/trellis2-and-pixal3d-are-now-native)). That fails the owner's no-quality-loss rule. Tencent's Hunyuan3D open weights state the licence "does not apply in the European Union", so they are unusable for an Austrian operator ([HF LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). Hosted TRELLIS.2 on fal is $0.30 at 1024p, no saving. FLUX 3 Image Klein weights are free to use commercially only below $5M revenue ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/black-forest-labs-opens-flux-3-image-klein-weights-with-free-commercial-use-limited-to-smaller-businesses)), irrelevant now but a future constraint.

## The 18+ end-user rule is cross-provider; ask fal in writing

fal's terms (updated 8 Sept 2026) say "you are responsible for ensuring that your End Users are at least 18 years of age", define End Users as customers who use the customer's solution, and bar exposing the APIs directly to End Users; no education or child carve-out was found ([fal Terms](https://fal.ai/terms)). Read literally, this conflicts with serving 11 to 14-year-olds, though students who only see cached, server-generated assets arguably do not "use" the API. Every other provider read has an adult-account-holder rule too: Gemini API requires 18+ and restricts clients "likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" (sentence remainder not captured), BFL requires 18+, and Replicate and OpenAI contemplate minors only with adult or parental consent ([Gemini terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms), [BFL Terms](https://bfl.ai/legal/terms-of-service), [Replicate Terms](https://replicate.com/terms), [OpenAI Business Terms](https://openai.com/policies/business-terms/)). Moving provider therefore does not solve this; Gemini direct is arguably worse. Tripo's terms returned HTTP 403 and were not read. This is not legal advice.

The concrete owner action is to email fal support and sales and ask for written confirmation, before the next paid usage, on four points. Does an education product whose end users are minors, who only view pre-generated, cached assets and never trigger or see generation requests, comply with Section 2? Can fal state whether Customer Input is used for model training (Section 6(c) allows Usage Data "to... offer... AI models" and gives no opt-out)? Where is the DPA, since `fal.ai/dpa` returned 404 although the terms incorporate it? Can zero data retention be confirmed beyond the `X-Fal-Store-IO: 0` header ([fal data retention](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/serverless/data-retention))? In parallel, keep student free text out of image and 3D prompts (generate only from curated concept names), and take legal advice on GDPR and child-data duties for an EU school product.

## Proposed A/B test (needs the owner's go-ahead; nothing has been run)

The test is a blind sheet across the real concepts: 10 topics, each run through four labelled-image models (current NB Pro $0.15, NB 2.1 about $0.04, GPT Image 2.5 Flare about $0.10 at an uncertain $0.05 to $0.21 range, Recraft V4.1 $0.035), for about $3.30. Score each on label spelling, label-to-object placement, no extra text, and match to the narration's cited labels. Add the 3D check: the 4 target objects through H3.1 standard texture ($1.20), plus H3.1 detailed texture ($1.60) if HD matters, with v2.5 outputs reused from the cache as baseline; and an optional Step 2 source sheet of 10 prompts across Schnell, Klein and Z-Image Turbo (about $0.17). **Estimated total is $6 to $8, hard cap $10 including reruns.** A challenger replaces NB Pro only if it ties or beats it on all four criteria on at least 9 of 10 topics; otherwise stay put. Prices for NB 2.1 on fal and GPT Image 2.5 Flare per-image cost are unconfirmed, so check fal's pricing API (free) before spending.

## Conclusion

The research reverses a natural assumption: the expensive-looking pipeline is already near the price floor for what it does, and the real exposure is not cost but staleness and compliance. Tripo v2.5 is a generation behind its own vendor at the same price, and fal's 18+ end-user language is a standing risk that costs nothing to clarify and could invalidate the stack. Image spelling accuracy is now commoditised across a dozen models, which means the sole defensible reason to keep paying $0.15 for NB Pro is the project's own diagram eval, so the A/B is the way to either confirm that or capture a roughly 4x saving on the labelled image without giving up quality.
